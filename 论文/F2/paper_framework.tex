\begin{abstract}
    Minimizing $W C_{\max}$ on a two-machine flow shop,
    denoted $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$, is a classical NP-hard scheduling problem.
    Existing exact methods rely on enumerative techniques that scale poorly with instance size,
    while approximation algorithms often impose restrictive assumptions on processing times.
    We first sequence all jobs globally by Johnson's rule, obtaining an initial schedule $\pi$.
    Scanning $\pi$ from left to right, we partition it into at most $K$ blocks: whenever a weight not seen before appears, 
    it serves as the final job of the current block, and the next job starts a new block.
    We establish structural properties of optimal schedules: the permutation property, the positional constraint that block $\mathcal{G}_k$ jobs cannot appear beyond the first $k$ blocks,
    and the block-end condition requiring the final job of each block to carry that block's weight (automatically satisfied by construction).
    The intra-block Johnson ordering inherited from $\pi$ is further formalized within the DP framework.
    Building on these properties and the interval-based dynamic programming framework (\citealp{hall1994}) developed for $F2|r_j|C_{\max}$,
    we design a polynomial-time dynamic programming algorithm with $O(|Y|_{\max}\cdot n)$ complexity that appends jobs block by block.
    We further show that geometric rounding of weights and processing times converts this algorithm into a polynomial-time approximation scheme.
\end{abstract}

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} Flow shop scheduling; $W C_{\max}$; Dynamic programming; Structural properties

\subsection{Problem Description}
We consider the problem $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$.
Let $\mathcal{J} = \{J_1, J_2, \dots, J_n\}$ be the set of $n$ jobs to be processed on two machines $M_1$ and $M_2$ in series:
 each job $J_j$ must be processed first on $M_1$ for $a_j$ time units and then on $M_2$ for $b_j$ time units, with no preemption allowed.
$M_1$ and $M_2$ can process at most one job at a time.
Each job $J_j$ has a weight $w_j > 0$.
A schedule $\pi = (\pi(1), \pi(2), \dots, \pi(n))$ specifies the processing order on both machines.
For a schedule $\pi$, let $A_j = \sum_{h=1}^{j} a_{\pi(h)}$ and $B_j = \sum_{h=1}^{j} b_{\pi(h)}$  be the cumulative $M_1$ and $M_2$ completion times after the first $j$ jobs.
The objective is to find a schedule $\pi$ that minimizes $W C_{\max}$.

For $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$, a permutation schedule in which the job order on $M_1$ and $M_2$, 
Possess an identical processing order for jobs (\citealp{baker1974introduction}).
Johnson's rule (\citealp{johnson1954optimal}) sequences all $n$ jobs to minimize the makespan:
jobs with $a_j \le b_j$ go first in non-decreasing order of $a_j$, and the remaining jobs follow
in non-increasing order of $b_j$.

For any scheduling $\pi$. Let $w_{(1)}, w_{(2)}, \dots, w_{(K)}$ be the $K$ distinct weights among all jobs, and $w_{(1)} > w_{(2)} > \dots > w_{(K)}$
and define $\mathcal{G}_k = \{ J_j \in \mathcal{J} \mid w_j = w^{(k)} \}$, $k = 1,\dots,K$.
For each group $\mathcal{G}_k$, let $\ell_k$ be the index of the last job of $\mathcal{G}_k$ appearing in $\pi$, 
and set $\ell_0 = 0$. The blocks are defined as $\mathcal{B}_k = \{\pi(\ell_{k-1}+1), \dots, \pi(\ell_k)\}$, $k = 1,\dots,K$.
Thus each block $\mathcal{B}_k$ ends with the last job of $\mathcal{G}_k$, and block weights are non-increasing:
$w(\mathcal{B}_1) \ge w(\mathcal{B}_2) \ge \cdots \ge w(\mathcal{B}_K)$.

For ease of explanation, we construct an illustrative schedule; all subsequent examples refer to this same instance.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:block-johnson}
For a given schedule  $\pi$, rescheduling each blocks to Johnson's ruler(\citealp{johnson1954optimal}) and 
concatenating blocks schedules delivers a schedule of length at most $WC(\pi)$
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By construction, the last job of block $\mathcal{B}_k$ carries weight $W(\mathcal{B}_k)$,
and every job in $\mathcal{B}_k$ has weight at most $W(\mathcal{B}_k)$,
since any weight appearing in $\mathcal{B}_k$ either equals $W(\mathcal{B}_k)$ or has already appeared in an earlier block with larger weight.
Let $C_{\max}^{(k)}(\sigma)$ be the completion time of the last job in $\mathcal{B}_k$ under schedule $\sigma$.
For any job $J_j \in \mathcal{B}_k$, its completion time satisfies $C_j(\sigma) \le C_{\max}^{(k)}(\sigma)$
and $w_j \le W(\mathcal{B}_k)$, hence
$w_j C_j(\sigma) \le W(\mathcal{B}_k) \cdot C_{\max}^{(k)}(\sigma)$.

Johnson's rule minimizes the makespan for $F2 \parallel C_{\max}$~\citep{johnson1954optimal}.
Let $\pi'$ be the schedule obtained by reordering each $\mathcal{B}_k$ internally by Johnson's rule
while preserving the block sequence.
We prove $C_{\max}^{(k)}(\pi') \le C_{\max}^{(k)}(\pi)$ for all $k$ by induction on $k$.

For $k=1$, block $\mathcal{B}_1$ starts at time zero on both machines under either schedule,
so internal Johnson ordering directly gives $C_{\max}^{(1)}(\pi') \le C_{\max}^{(1)}(\pi)$.
Assume $C_{\max}^{(k-1)}(\pi') \le C_{\max}^{(k-1)}(\pi)$.
On $M_1$, the start time of $\mathcal{B}_k$ equals the total $M_1$ work of $\mathcal{B}_1,\dots,\mathcal{B}_{k-1}$,
which is unchanged by internal reordering, so $\mathcal{B}_k$ starts at the same time on $M_1$ under both schedules.
On $M_2$, $\mathcal{B}_k$ starts at $\max\{\text{its own $M_1$ completion},\; C_{\max}^{(k-1)}\}$;
the first term is identical and the second is no larger under $\pi'$ by the induction hypothesis.
Thus $\mathcal{B}_k$ starts no later on $M_2$ under $\pi'$, and with the same $M_1$ start and internal Johnson ordering,
$C_{\max}^{(k)}(\pi') \le C_{\max}^{(k)}(\pi)$.

Therefore
$WC_{\max}(\pi') = \max_{j} w_j C_j(\pi')
\le \max_{k} W(\mathcal{B}_k) \cdot C_{\max}^{(k)}(\pi')
\le \max_{k} W(\mathcal{B}_k) \cdot C_{\max}^{(k)}(\pi)
\le WC_{\max}(\pi)$,
where the last inequality holds because for the job $J^*$ achieving $WC_{\max}(\pi)$,
letting $\mathcal{B}_{k^*}$ be its block, $WC_{\max}(\pi) = w_{J^*} C_{J^*}(\pi) = W(\mathcal{B}_{k^*}) \cdot C_{\max}^{(k^*)}(\pi)$.
\end{proof} 


\subsection{Mixed Integer Linear Programming Formulation}
The problem $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ can be formulated as a mixed integer linear program (MILP).
For a permutation schedule, let the positions be indexed by $k = 1,\dots,n$.
We introduce the following decision variables:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $x_{jk} \in \{0,1\}$ --- equals $1$ if job $J_j$ occupies position $k$, and $0$ otherwise;
	\item $C_k^1 \ge 0$ --- completion time of the job at position $k$ on $M_1$;
	\item $C_k^2 \ge 0$ --- completion time of the job at position $k$ on $M_2$;
	\item $C_j \ge 0$ --- completion time of job $J_j$ on $M_2$;
	\item $Z \ge 0$ --- the maximum weighted completion time $W C_{\max}$.
	\item $M = \sum_{j=1}^{n}(a_j + b_j)$ be a sufficiently large constant.
\end{itemize}
The MILP model is:
\begin{align}
	\min \quad & Z \label{milp:obj} \\[4pt]
	\text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_{jk} = 1, \quad \forall\, j \in \mathcal{J} \label{milp:assign1} \\[4pt]
	& \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_{jk} = 1, \quad \forall\, k = 1,\dots,n \label{milp:assign2} \\[4pt]
	& C_1^1 = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_j x_{j1} \label{milp:m1_start} \\[4pt]
	& C_k^1 = C_{k-1}^1 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_j x_{jk}, \quad \forall\, k = 2,\dots,n \label{milp:m1_rec} \\[4pt]
	& C_1^2 = C_1^1 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} b_j x_{j1} \label{milp:m2_start} \\[4pt]
	& C_k^2 \ge C_k^1 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} b_j x_{jk}, \quad \forall\, k = 2,\dots,n \label{milp:m2_a} \\[4pt]
	& C_k^2 \ge C_{k-1}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} b_j x_{jk}, \quad \forall\, k = 2,\dots,n \label{milp:m2_b} \\[4pt]
	& C_j \ge C_k^2 - M(1 - x_{jk}), \quad \forall\, j \in \mathcal{J},\; k = 1,\dots,n \label{milp:link} \\[4pt]
	& Z \ge w_j C_j, \quad \forall\, j \in \mathcal{J} \label{milp:wcmax} \\[4pt]
	& x_{jk} \in \{0,1\},\quad C_k^1, C_k^2, C_j, Z \ge 0 \label{milp:dom}
\end{align}

Constraint (\ref{milp:assign1})--(\ref{milp:assign2}) ensure a one-to-one assignment between jobs and positions.
Constraints (\ref{milp:m1_start})--(\ref{milp:m1_rec}) compute the completion time of each position on $M_1$.
Constraints (\ref{milp:m2_start})--(\ref{milp:m2_b}) compute the completion time of each position on $M_2$:
for $k \ge 2$, the job at position $k$ starts on $M_2$ only after it completes $M_1$ and the preceding job releases $M_2$.
Constraint (\ref{milp:link}) links the position-based completion times to the job-based completion times via a big-$M$ formulation:
when $x_{jk} = 1$, job $J_j$ is at position $k$ and $C_j \ge C_k^2$;
when $x_{jk} = 0$, the right-hand side becomes $C_k^2 - M \le 0$, which is non-binding.
Constraint (\ref{milp:wcmax}) enforces $Z \ge w_j C_j$ for every job, so that minimizing $Z$ yields the optimal $W C_{\max}$.




\section{Problem formulation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nphard}
    $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ is NP-hard.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We reduce from the Partition problem: given $n$ positive integers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_r$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{r} x_i = 2T$, 
does there exist subset $Z_1$ and $Z_2 \subseteq \{1,\dots,r\}$ such that:
 $\sum_{i \in Z_1} x_i$ = $\sum_{i \in Z_2} x_i$ = $T$?

Construct an instance of $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$,  Let the number of jobs $n = r + 1$.
Let $a_j = 1$, $b_j = rx_j$, $w_j = T+1$ for jobs $j = 1,\dots,n-1$.
Put $a_n = rT$, $b_n = 1$, $w_n = 2T+1$ for job $J_n$.
Does there exist a threshold Y such that:
\[
W C_{\max} \le Y = r(T+1)(2T+1)
\]
holds?

We partition jobs $j_1$ to $j_{n-1}$ into two subsets via $j_n$, 
the distribution of jobs on the machine is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:3-1}.
Since the processing times of $A_1$ and $A_2$ are unknown, while their total processing time is 2T. 
We may assume that the processing time of $A_1$ on $M_2$ is $r(T+\xi)$, $A_2$ is $r(T-\xi)$, where $-T\le\xi\le T$.
Therefore, the completion time of $J_n$ is determined by the following formula:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completetime1}
    \qquad
    C_n^1 = |A_1| + rT + 1
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completetime2}
    \qquad
    C_n^2 = 1 + r ( T + \xi ) + 1
\end{equation}
and $C_n = \max \{C_n^1, C_n^2\}$

If $\xi = 0$, then $\sum_{j\epsilon A_1}b_j =\sum_{j\epsilon A_2}b_j $, which means the partition problem has a feasible solution. 
In this case, the completion time of job \(J_n\) is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime1}, we have:
\begin{align*}
C_n &= |A_1| + rT + 1 \leqslant (rT + r) = r(T+1) \\
w_nC_n &= (2T + 1)(r(T+1)) = Y\\
W C_{\max} &= r(T+1)(C_n + rT) = Y\\
\end{align*}
so $w_jC_j \leqslant W C_{\max} = Y$

If the scheduling problem exists a threshold y such that \(W C_{\text{max}} \le y\).
When $ \xi > 0 $, $C_n$is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime2}, we have:
\begin{align*}
C_n &= (1 + r ( T + \xi ) + 1) \geqslant (1 + r ( T + 1 ) + 1) \\
w_nC_n  &\geqslant (2T + 1)(1 + r ( T + 1 ) + 1) = Y
\end{align*}
When $ \xi < 0 $, $C_n$is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime1}, we have:
\begin{align*}
C_{\max} &= (C_n +  \sum_{j\epsilon A_2}b_j)  \\
         &= (1 + rT + |A_1|  + r ( T - \xi )) \\
         &\geqslant (rT + r + rT)  \\
         &= r(2T+1) = Y \\
WC_{\max} &\geqslant r(T+1)(r(2T+1)) = Y\\
\end{align*}
so $W C_{\max} > Y$, there exists a contradiction.

Therefore, the Partition instance admits a solution if and only if 
the constructed $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ instance admits a schedule with $W C_{\max} \le Y$.
\end{proof}



\begin{theorem}\label{thm:snphard}
    $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ is strongly NP-hard.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We reduce from the 3-Partition problem: given $3m$ positive integers $x_1,\dots,x_{3m}$ and a bound $B$ with
$B/4 < x_i < B/2$ and $\sum_{i=1}^{3m} x_i = mB$, can the integers be partitioned into $m$ triples each summing to $B$?
Construct an instance of $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ with $n = 4m + 1$ jobs as follows (see Figure~\ref{fig:3-2}).
Let $M = n$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $3m$ element jobs $J_1,\dots,J_{3m}$: $a_j = 0$, $b_j = x_j$, $w_j = 1$;
  \item $m$ separator jobs $S_1,\dots,S_{m}$: $a_{s_k} = B$, $b_{s_k} = 0$, $w_{s_k} = M \cdot m / k$;
  \item one terminal job $T$: $a_T = 0$, $b_T = 0$, $w_T = M$.
\end{itemize}
Element jobs and $T$ have $a_j = 0$, so they occupy no processing time on $M_1$;
symmetrically, separators and $T$ have $b_j = 0$, occupying no time on $M_2$.
Thus the total $M_1$ work is $\sum a_j = mB$ (solely from the $m$ separators),
and the total $M_2$ work is $\sum b_j = mB$ (solely from the $3m$ element jobs).
Define the threshold $Y = M \cdot mB$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{($\Rightarrow$)} Suppose a 3-Partition exists, i.e., there is a partition of the $3m$ elements into
$m$ triples $G_1,\dots,G_m$ with $\sum_{J_j \in G_k} x_j = B$ for each $k$.
Arrange the jobs in the order $G_1, S_1, G_2, S_2, \dots, G_m, S_m, T$.
Since $a_j = 0$ for element jobs and $T$, on $M_1$ the separators occupy
$[0,B], [B,2B], \dots, [(m-1)B, mB]$ without interruption;
$C_{s_k}^1 = kB$ for each $k$, and $C_T^1 = mB$.
On $M_2$, element jobs in $G_1$ start at time~$0$ and, by the partition property,
each $G_k$ finishes at time $kB$, which equals the $M_1$ completion of $S_k$.
Hence $M_2$ has no idle time.
The separator $S_k$ passes through $M_2$ instantly at time $kB$, so $C_{s_k} = kB$,
and the terminal job starts on $M_2$ at $\max\{mB,\, mB\} = mB$, yielding $C_T = mB$.
Consequently
\[
w_T C_T = M \cdot mB = Y,\qquad
w_{s_k} C_{s_k} = \frac{M m}{k} \cdot kB = M mB = Y,\qquad
w_j C_j \le 1 \cdot mB < Y\;(M>1).
\]
Thus $W C_{\max} = Y$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{($\Leftarrow$)} Suppose there exists a schedule $\pi$ with $W C_{\max} \le Y = M \cdot mB$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Ordering of separators.}\quad
For any separator $S_j$ (with weight $M m / j$), its weighted completion satisfies
$w_{s_j} C_{s_j} \le M mB$, hence $C_{s_j} \le jB$.
On $M_1$, each separator requires $B$ time units; if $S_j$ were the $k$-th separator on $M_1$ (with $k > j$),
its $M_1$ completion would be at least $k B > jB$, contradicting $C_{s_j} \le jB$.
Thus each $S_j$ must occupy position $j$ among the separators on $M_1$, forcing the order
$S_1, S_2, \dots, S_m$ and the $M_1$ timeline $[0,B], [B,2B], \dots, [(m-1)B, mB]$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Bounding $C_T$.}\quad
Since $w_T = M$, we have $M \cdot C_T \le M mB$, i.e., $C_T \le mB$.
The total $M_2$ work of all element jobs is $mB$, and every separator and $T$ has $b_j = 0$,
so $C_T \ge mB$. Hence $C_T = mB$ and $M_2$ has zero idle time before $T$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Partition structure.}\quad
Define $G_k$ as the set of element jobs scheduled between $S_{k-1}$ and $S_k$ in $\pi$
(with $S_0$ denoting the start), and let $G_{m+1}$ be those after $S_m$ and before $T$.
Set $s_k = \sum_{J_j \in G_k} x_j$; then $\sum_{k=1}^{m+1} s_k = mB$.
All jobs in $G_k$ finish $M_1$ at time $(k-1)B$ (after $S_{k-1}$).

On $M_2$, let $E_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} s_i$ be the cumulative work of the first $k$ groups.
Because $M_2$ has zero idle time, after processing $G_k$ and the (instantaneous) $S_k$,
the $M_2$ clock reads $\max\{k B,\; E_k\}$.
For $M_2$ to remain continuously busy, we must have $E_k \ge k B$ for every $k=1,\dots,m$;
otherwise $M_2$ would finish the first $k$ groups before time $k B$ and wait for
$G_{k+1}$ to become $M_1$-ready (at time $k B$).

On the other hand, $C_{s_k} = \max\{k B,\; E_k\} \le k B$ (from $w_{s_k} C_{s_k} \le Y$),
forcing $E_k \le k B$.
Together we obtain $E_k = k B$ for all $k = 1,\dots,m$.
In particular $s_{m+1} = mB - E_m = mB - mB = 0$, so $G_{m+1} = \emptyset$
and all element jobs are placed before $S_m$.
Moreover $s_k = E_k - E_{k-1} = k B - (k-1) B = B$ for every $k = 1,\dots,m$.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Group sizes.}\quad
The 3-Partition bounds $B/4 < x_i < B/2$ imply that each $G_k$ contains exactly three elements:
with fewer than three elements the sum would be at most $2 \cdot (B/2 - \varepsilon) < B$,
while four or more elements would sum to at least $4 \cdot (B/4 + \varepsilon) > B$.
Thus $\{G_1,\dots,G_m\}$ is a partition of the $3m$ elements into $m$ triples each summing to $B$,
yielding a valid 3-Partition.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$}{F2 || W C\_max} for the number of weights is constant} 
\label{sec:dp_algorithm}
In this chapter, we will propose an idea to guide the dynamic programming algorithm for solving this problem, 
and present the time complexity analysis of the algorithm.

When the number of distinct weights $K$ is bounded by a constant, 
the structural properties established in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} enable a polynomial-time dynamic programming algorithm.
The initial job sequence follows Johnson's rule; 
the block partition and all subsequent steps are defined with respect to this Johnson order.
We first describe the block partition and group logic underlying our approach, 
then present the DP formulation, and finally give the complete algorithm and its complexity analysis.

\subsection{Algorithm Properties and Dynamic Programming}


We now develop a dynamic programming algorithm that processes jobs sequentially in Johnson order
and assigns each job to one of the $K$ blocks $\mathcal{B}_1,\dots,\mathcal{B}_K$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:block-johnson} provides the structural basis:
it guarantees that, given any assignment of jobs to the $K$ blocks,
sequencing the jobs within each block $\mathcal{B}_i$ according to Johnson's rule
and concatenating the blocks in non-increasing weight order yields a schedule
whose makespan is no larger than that of any other schedule respecting the same assignment.
We denote by $\mathscr{H}$ the set of states maintained during the computation;
each state summarizes the aggregate parameters of all $K$ blocks after a prefix of jobs has been processed.
A state is a $4K$-tuple
\[
    (C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}
\]
for $\mathcal{B}_i$, $i = 1,\dots,K$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $A_i$ --- total processing time on machine $M_1$ of jobs in block $\mathcal{B}_i$;
    \item $B_i$ --- total processing time on machine $M_2$ of jobs in block $\mathcal{B}_i$;
    \item $C_i$ --- makespan of block $\mathcal{B}_i$ when the jobs assigned to it are scheduled in isolation;
    \item $L_i$ --- weight of the last job in block $\mathcal{B}_i$.
\end{itemize}

The initial state set contains a single state with all components set to zero:
$\mathscr{H}^{(0)} = \{ (0,\dots,0) \}$.


\paragraph{State Transition.}  For $j =1..... n$ perform the following computations. 
Set $Y_i \gets \emptyset, \quad i = 1, \dots, k$. For each tuple
$(C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}$
and $i = 1..... K$, if $w_j \leqslant  L_i$ do the following.
\begin{align*}
    Y_i \gets Y_i \cup \bigl\{\,
    \bigl( C_1, \dots, C_{i-1},\,\max\{C_i + b_j,\; A_i + a_j + b_j\},\,C_{i+1}, \dots, C_K,\; \\
    A_1, \dots, A_{i-1},\,A_i + a_j,\,A_{i+1}, \dots, A_K,\;\\
    B_1, \dots, B_{i-1},\,B_i + b_j,\,B_{i+1}, \dots, B_K \bigr)
\,\bigr\}
\end{align*}
Merge $i = 1,...,K$, to obtain $\mathscr{H}^{(j)}$. 
If we get the same tuples during the merge, store only one of them.

Once all jobs are assigned and each block $\mathcal{B}_i$ is summarized by its parameters $(A_i, B_i, C_i)$,
the overall makespan $C_{\max}$ is obtained by concatenating the blocks in order of increasing index.
The procedure is given below:

In the update of $T_B$,
the term $T_A - A_i$ is the start time of $\mathcal{B}_i$ on $M_1$, so $T_A - A_i + C_i$
is the completion time of $\mathcal{B}_i$ on $M_2$ when $M_2$ is free,
while $T_B + B_i$ accounts for the case where $M_2$ is the bottleneck.


\setlength{\intextsep}{4pt}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Block concatenation}
\label{alg:Block-concat}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \State \textbf{Input:} Block parameters $(A_i, B_i, C_i)$ for $i = 1,\dots,K$.
    \State \textbf{Output:} Completion time $C$ on $M_2$.
    \State $T_{A_0} \gets 0$, \enspace $T_{B_0} \gets 0$
    \For{$i = 1$ to $K$}
        \State $T_{A_i} \gets T_{A_{i-1}} + A_i$
        \State $T_{B_i} \gets \max\{\, T_{A_{i-1}} + C_i,\; T_{B_{i-1}} + B_i \,\}$
    \EndFor
    \State $C \gets T_{B_K}$
    \State \Return $C$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{DP for $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$}
\label{alg:dp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \State \textbf{Input:} Jobs $\mathcal{J}$ with $(a_j, b_j, w_j)$ in Johnson order; block count $K$.
    \State \textbf{Output:} The optimal value $Z^* = \min_S \max_i\,L_i\cdot T_B^{(i)}$.
    \State $\mathscr{H}^{(0)} \gets \{ (0,\dots,0) \}$
    \For{$j = 1$ to $n$}
        \State $Y_i \gets \emptyset$ for $i = 1,\dots,K$
        \For{each $(\{C_i,A_i,B_i,L_i\}_{i=1}^K) \in \mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}$}
            \For{$i = 1$ to $K$}
                \If{$w_j \le L_i$}
                    \State $C_i' \gets \max\{\, C_i + b_j,\; A_i + a_j + b_j \,\}$
                    \State $A_i' \gets A_i + a_j$, \enspace $B_i' \gets B_i + b_j$
                    \State Add $(\dots,C_i',\dots; \dots,A_i',\dots; \dots,B_i',\dots; \dots,L_i,\dots)$ to $Y_i$
                \EndIf
            \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \State $\mathscr{H}^{(j)} \gets \textsc{Merge}(Y_1,\dots,Y_K)$
    \EndFor
    \State $Z^* \gets +\infty$
    \For{each state $S = \{C_i, A_i, B_i, L_i\}_{i=1}^K \in \mathscr{H}^{(n)}$}
        \For{$i = 1$ to $K$}
            \State $C{(S)} \gets \textsc{Algorithm~\ref{alg:Block-concat}}\bigl((A_1,B_1,C_1),\dots,(A_i,B_i,C_i)\bigr)$
        \EndFor
        \State $W C_{\max} \gets \max_{i=1,\dots,K}\; L_i \cdot C{(S)}$,
        \State $Z^* \gets \min\{\, Z^*,\; W C_{\max} \,\}$
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $Z^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}




\begin{theorem}\label{thm:complexity}
Algorithm~2 solves $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ optimally in
$O\bigl(n \cdot K \cdot |\mathscr{H}|_{\max}\bigr)$ time, where
$|\mathscr{H}|_{\max} \le K^K \cdot V^{\,3K-2}$,
$V = \max\bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^n a_j,\;\sum_{j=1}^n b_j\bigr\}$.
For constant $K$, this is $O(n \cdot V^{\,3K-2})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each state in $\mathscr{H}^{(j)}$ is a $4K$-tuple
$(C_1,\dots,C_K;\, A_1,\dots,A_K;\, B_1,\dots,B_K;\, L_1,\dots,L_K)$.
We bound the number of distinct values each component can attain.

The quantity $A_i$ is the total $M_1$ processing time of jobs assigned to block $\mathcal{B}_i$,
hence $A_i \in [0,\,V]$.
Since the job sequence is fixed and the $j$ jobs under consideration form a prefix of the Johnson order,
$\sum_{i=1}^K A_i$ equals the sum of $a_j$ over that prefix.
Consequently at most $K-1$ of the $A_i$ are independent, contributing at most $V^{\,K-1}$ distinct $A$-vectors.
Symmetrically, $\sum_{i=1}^K B_i$ is the prefix sum of the $b_j$'s,
so the $B_i$ components likewise contribute at most $V^{\,K-1}$ distinct combinations.

The quantity $C_i$ is the makespan of $\mathcal{B}_i$ when scheduled in isolation by Johnson's rule.
Since $C_i \le A_i + B_i \le 2V$, the $C$-vector contributes at most $(2V)^K$ distinct values.
The weight $L_i$ is the weight of the last job in $\mathcal{B}_i$ and takes values from $\{w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(K)}\}$,
yielding at most $K^K$ distinct $L$-vectors.

Multiplying these bounds yields
\[
|\mathscr{H}^{(j)}| \le K^K \cdot (2V)^K \cdot V^{\,2K-2}
                      = O\bigl(K^K \cdot 2^K \cdot V^{\,3K-2}\bigr).
\]

In iteration $j$, the algorithm takes each state in $\mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}$, tries every
block $i \in \{1,\dots,K\}$, computes the updated tuple in $O(1)$ time,
and inserts it into $Y_i$.
The merge step collects the $K$ sets $Y_1,\dots,Y_K$ and removes duplicates in time
proportional to the total number of generated tuples.
Hence iteration $j$ costs $O(K \cdot |\mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}|)$,
and summing over all $n$ iterations gives $O(n \cdot K \cdot |\mathscr{H}|_{\max})$.

The final concatenation via Algorithm~1 evaluates each state in $\mathscr{H}^{(n)}$
in $O(K)$ time, which is dominated by the DP phase.
When $K$ is fixed, $K^K \cdot 2^K$ is constant, giving $O(n \cdot V^{\,3K-2})$.
\end{proof}
