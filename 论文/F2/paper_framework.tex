\begin{abstract}
    Minimizing $W C_{\max}$ on a two-machine flow shop,
    denoted $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$, is a classical NP-hard scheduling problem.
    Existing exact methods rely on enumerative techniques that scale poorly with instance size,
    while approximation algorithms often impose restrictive assumptions on processing times.
    We first sequence all jobs globally by Johnson's rule, obtaining an initial schedule $\pi$.
    Scanning $\pi$ from left to right, we partition it into at most $K$ blocks: whenever a weight not seen before appears, 
    it serves as the final job of the current block, and the next job starts a new block.
    We establish structural properties of optimal schedules: the permutation property, the positional constraint that block $\mathcal{G}_k$ jobs cannot appear beyond the first $k$ blocks,
    and the block-end condition requiring the final job of each block to carry that block's weight (automatically satisfied by construction).
    The intra-block Johnson ordering inherited from $\pi$ is further formalized within the DP framework.
    Building on these properties and the interval-based dynamic programming framework (\citealp{hall1994}) developed for $F2|r_j|C_{\max}$,
    we design a polynomial-time dynamic programming algorithm with $O(|Y|_{\max}\cdot n)$ complexity that appends jobs block by block.
    We further show that geometric rounding of weights and processing times converts this algorithm into a polynomial-time approximation scheme.
\end{abstract}

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} Flow shop scheduling; $W C_{\max}$; Dynamic programming; Structural properties

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

We consider the problem $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$.
Let $\mathcal{J} = \{J_1, J_2, \dots, J_n\}$ be the set of $n$ jobs to be processed on two machines $M_1$ and $M_2$ in series:
 each job $J_j$ must be processed first on $M_1$ for $a_j$ time units and then on $M_2$ for $b_j$ time units, with no preemption allowed.
$M_1$ and $M_2$ can process at most one job at a time.
Each job $J_j$ has a weight $w_j > 0$.
A schedule $\pi = (\pi(1), \pi(2), \dots, \pi(n))$ specifies the processing order on both machines.
For a schedule $\pi$, let $A_j = \sum_{h=1}^{j} a_{\pi(h)}$ and $B_j = \sum_{h=1}^{j} b_{\pi(h)}$  be the cumulative $M_1$ and $M_2$ completion times after the first $j$ jobs.

The objective is to find a schedule $\pi$ that minimizes $W C_{\max}$.



For $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$, a permutation schedule in which the job order on $M_1$ and $M_2$, 
Possess an identical processing order for jobs (\citealp{baker1974introduction}).
Johnson's rule (\citealp{johnson1954optimal}) sequences all $n$ jobs to minimize the makespan:
jobs with $a_j \le b_j$ go first in non-decreasing order of $a_j$, and the remaining jobs follow
in non-increasing order of $b_j$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:johnson}
For $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$, there exists an optimal schedule in which all jobs are sequenced according to Johnson's rule.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\textbf{Proof.}
Consider any optimal schedule $\pi^*$ for $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$.
If the jobs in $\pi^*$ are already in Johnson order, we are done.
Otherwise, there exist two adjacent jobs $J_u$ and $J_v$ (with $J_u$ immediately preceding $J_v$)
that violate Johnson's rule, i.e., $\min\{a_u, b_v\} > \min\{a_v, b_u\}$.

Let $\pi'$ be the schedule obtained by swapping $J_u$ and $J_v$.
For any permutation schedule $\sigma$, its makespan can be expressed as
\[
C(\sigma)=\max_{1\le r\le n}\Bigl\{\sum_{h=1}^{r} a_{\sigma(h)} + \sum_{h=r}^{n} b_{\sigma(h)}\Bigr\}.
\]
A standard exchange argument shows that when $\min\{a_u, b_v\} > \min\{a_v, b_u\}$, swapping $J_u$ and $J_v$ does not increase
the makespan, i.e., $C(\pi') \le C(\pi^*)$.
Moreover, since the two jobs are adjacent on both machines and the swap only affects these two positions,
all other jobs finish no later in $\pi'$ than in $\pi^*$.
Hence for every job $J_j$, its completion time $C_j(\pi') \le C_j(\pi^*)$,
which implies $w_j C_j(\pi') \le w_j C_j(\pi^*)$ for all $j$.
Therefore $W C_{\max}(\pi') \le W C_{\max}(\pi^*)$.
By repeatedly applying this exchange argument to adjacent inversions,
any schedule can be transformed into a Johnson-ordered schedule
without increasing the objective value.
Thus, there exists an optimal schedule in which all jobs are sequenced according to Johnson's rule.
\hfill$\Box$


\subsection{Mixed Integer Linear Programming Formulation}

The problem $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ can be formulated as a mixed integer linear program (MILP).
For a permutation schedule, let the positions be indexed by $k = 1,\dots,n$.
We introduce the following decision variables:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $x_{jk} \in \{0,1\}$ --- equals $1$ if job $J_j$ occupies position $k$, and $0$ otherwise;
	\item $C_k^1 \ge 0$ --- completion time of the job at position $k$ on $M_1$;
	\item $C_k^2 \ge 0$ --- completion time of the job at position $k$ on $M_2$;
	\item $C_j \ge 0$ --- completion time of job $J_j$ on $M_2$;
	\item $Z \ge 0$ --- the maximum weighted completion time $W C_{\max}$.
\end{itemize}

Let $M = \sum_{j=1}^{n}(a_j + b_j)$ be a sufficiently large constant.
The MILP model is:

\begin{align}
	\min \quad & Z \label{milp:obj} \\[4pt]
	\text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{k=1}^{n} x_{jk} = 1, \quad \forall\, j \in \mathcal{J} \label{milp:assign1} \\[4pt]
	& \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_{jk} = 1, \quad \forall\, k = 1,\dots,n \label{milp:assign2} \\[4pt]
	& C_1^1 = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_j x_{j1} \label{milp:m1_start} \\[4pt]
	& C_k^1 = C_{k-1}^1 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_j x_{jk}, \quad \forall\, k = 2,\dots,n \label{milp:m1_rec} \\[4pt]
	& C_1^2 = C_1^1 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} b_j x_{j1} \label{milp:m2_start} \\[4pt]
	& C_k^2 \ge C_k^1 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} b_j x_{jk}, \quad \forall\, k = 2,\dots,n \label{milp:m2_a} \\[4pt]
	& C_k^2 \ge C_{k-1}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{n} b_j x_{jk}, \quad \forall\, k = 2,\dots,n \label{milp:m2_b} \\[4pt]
	& C_j \ge C_k^2 - M(1 - x_{jk}), \quad \forall\, j \in \mathcal{J},\; k = 1,\dots,n \label{milp:link} \\[4pt]
	& Z \ge w_j C_j, \quad \forall\, j \in \mathcal{J} \label{milp:wcmax} \\[4pt]
	& x_{jk} \in \{0,1\},\quad C_k^1, C_k^2, C_j, Z \ge 0 \label{milp:dom}
\end{align}

Constraint (\ref{milp:assign1})--(\ref{milp:assign2}) ensure a one-to-one assignment between jobs and positions.
Constraints (\ref{milp:m1_start})--(\ref{milp:m1_rec}) compute the completion time of each position on $M_1$.
Constraints (\ref{milp:m2_start})--(\ref{milp:m2_b}) compute the completion time of each position on $M_2$:
for $k \ge 2$, the job at position $k$ starts on $M_2$ only after it completes $M_1$ and the preceding job releases $M_2$.
Constraint (\ref{milp:link}) links the position-based completion times to the job-based completion times via a big-$M$ formulation:
when $x_{jk} = 1$, job $J_j$ is at position $k$ and $C_j \ge C_k^2$;
when $x_{jk} = 0$, the right-hand side becomes $C_k^2 - M \le 0$, which is non-binding.
Constraint (\ref{milp:wcmax}) enforces $Z \ge w_j C_j$ for every job, so that minimizing $Z$ yields the optimal $W C_{\max}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nphard}
    $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ is NP-hard.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We reduce from the Partition problem: given $n$ positive integers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_r$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{r} x_i = 2T$, 
does there exist subset $Z_1$ and $Z_2 \subseteq \{1,\dots,r\}$ such that:
 $\sum_{i \in Z_1} x_i$ = $\sum_{i \in Z_2} x_i$ = $T$?

Construct an instance of $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$,  Let the number of jobs $n = r + 1$.
Let $a_j = 1$, $b_j = rx_j$, $w_j = T+1$ for jobs $j = 1,\dots,n-1$.
Put $a_n = rT$, $b_n = 1$, $w_n = 2T+1$ for job $J_n$.
Does there exist a threshold Y such that:
\[
W C_{\max} \le Y = r(T+1)(2T+1)
\]
holds?

We partition jobs $j_1$ to $j_{n-1}$ into two subsets via $j_n$, 
the distribution of jobs on the machine is($\mathcal{J}_{j\epsilon A_1}, J_n, \mathcal{J}_{j\epsilon A_2}$). 
Since the processing times of $A_1$ and $A_2$ are unknown, while their total processing time is 2T. 
We may assume that the processing time of $A_1$ on $M_2$ is $r(T+\xi)$, $A_2$ is $r(T-\xi)$, where $-T\le\xi\le T$.
Therefore, the completion time of $J_n$ is determined by the following formula:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completetime1}
    \qquad
    C_n^1 = |A_1| + rT + 1
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completetime2}
    \qquad
    C_n^2 = 1 + r ( T + \xi ) + 1
\end{equation}
and $C_n = \max \{C_n^1, C_n^2\}$

If $\xi = 0$, then $\sum_{j\epsilon A_1}b_j =\sum_{j\epsilon A_2}b_j $, which means the partition problem has a feasible solution. 
In this case, the completion time of job \(J_n\) is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime1}, we have:
\begin{align*}
w_nC_n &= (2T+1)(|A_1| + rT + 1) \\
       &\leqslant (2T+1)(rT + r) \\
       &= r(T+1)(2T+1) \\
       &= Y\\
W C_{\max} &= r(T+1)(C_n + rT) = Y
\end{align*}
so $w_jC_j \leqslant W C_{\max} = Y$

If the scheduling problem exists a threshold y such that \(W C_{\text{max}} \le y\).
When $ \xi > 0 $, $C_n$is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime2}, we have:
\begin{align*}
w_nC_n &= (1 + r ( T + \xi ) + 1)(2T + 1) \\
        &\geqslant (1 + r ( T + 1 ) + 1)(2T + 1) \\
        &> Y
\end{align*}
When $ \xi < 0 $, $C_n$is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime1}, we have:
\begin{align*}
W C_{\max} &= (C_n +  \sum_{j\epsilon A_2}b_j)(T+1)  \\
         &= (1 + rT + |A_1|  + r ( T - \xi ))(T+1) \\
         &\geqslant (rT + r + rT)(T+1)  \\
         &= r(T+1)(2T+1) = Y
\end{align*}
so $W C_{\max} > Y$, there exists a contradiction.


Therefore, the Partition instance admits a solution if and only if 
the constructed $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ instance admits a schedule with $W C_{\max} \le Y$.
    
\end{proof}



\section{\texorpdfstring{$F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$}{F2 || W C\_max} for the number of weights is constant} 
\label{sec:dp_algorithm}

When the number of distinct weights $K$ is bounded by a constant, 
the structural properties established in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} enable a polynomial-time dynamic programming algorithm.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:johnson}, the initial job sequence follows Johnson's rule; 
the block partition and all subsequent steps are defined with respect to this Johnson order.
We first describe the block partition and group logic underlying our approach, 
then present the DP formulation, and finally give the complete algorithm and its complexity analysis.

\subsection{Algorithm Properties and Dynamic Programming}

Starting from the first job, a new block is created whenever a weight value not encountered before appears, 
that job becomes the final job of the current block, and the next job starts a new block. 
Jobs of the same weight form a natural group, denoted $\mathcal{G}_1, \mathcal{G}_2, \dots, \mathcal{G}_K$. 
Since each block introduces at least one previously unseen weight, the procedure yields at most $K$ blocks, 
denoted $\mathcal{B}_1, \mathcal{B}_2, \dots, \mathcal{B}_K$. By construction, 
the final job of each block carries the weight that triggered the cut.
By construction, block weights are non-increasing, 
i.e., $W(\mathcal{B}_1) \ge W(\mathcal{B}_2) \ge \cdots \ge W(\mathcal{B}_K)$. 


\begin{exam}\label{ex:blocking}
Consider a 6-job instance with Johnson sequence $\pi = (J_1, \dots, J_6)$ and jobs with (aj, bj, wj) values: 
$(J_1,\dots,J_6) = {(2, 5, 2); (5, 7, 2); (6, 9, 5); (8, 4, 2); (4, 3, 4); (3, 2 ,2);}$.

Scanning $\pi$ from left to right and cutting whenever a weight not seen before appears yields the following partition:
The resulting blocks are $\mathcal{B}_1=\{J_1,J_2,J_3\}$, $\mathcal{B}_2=\{J_4,J_5\}$, $\mathcal{B}_3=\{J_6\}$. 
And $\mathcal{G}_1=\{J_3\}$, $\mathcal{G}_2=\{J_5\}$, $\mathcal{G}_3=\{J_1,J_2,J_4,J_6\}$.
\end{exam}

\label{sec:dp_formulation}

We now develop a dynamic programming algorithm that processes jobs sequentially in Johnson order
and assigns each job to one of the $K$ blocks $\mathcal{B}_1,\dots,\mathcal{B}_K$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:block-johnson} provides the structural basis:
it guarantees that, given any assignment of jobs to the $K$ blocks,
sequencing the jobs within each block $\mathcal{B}_i$ according to Johnson's rule
and concatenating the blocks in non-increasing weight order yields a schedule
whose makespan is no larger than that of any other schedule respecting the same assignment.
We denote by $\mathscr{H}$ the set of states maintained during the computation;
each state summarizes the aggregate parameters of all $K$ blocks after a prefix of jobs has been processed.

\paragraph{State Definition.}

A state is a $4K$-tuple
\[
    (C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}
\]
for $\mathcal{B}_i$, $i = 1,\dots,K$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $A_i$ --- total processing time on machine $M_1$ of jobs in block $\mathcal{B}_i$;
    \item $B_i$ --- total processing time on machine $M_2$ of jobs in block $\mathcal{B}_i$;
    \item $C_i$ --- makespan of block $\mathcal{B}_i$ when the jobs assigned to it are scheduled in isolation;
    \item $L_i$ --- weight of the last job in block $\mathcal{B}_i$.
\end{itemize}

The initial state set contains a single state with all components set to zero:
$\mathscr{H}^{(0)} = \{ (0,\dots,0) \}$.


\paragraph{State Transition.}  For $j =1..... n$ perform the following computations. 
Set $Y_i \gets \emptyset, \quad i = 1, \dots, k$. For each tuple
$(C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}$
and $i = 1..... K$, if $w_j \leqslant  L_i$ do the following.
\begin{align*}
    Y_i \gets Y_i \cup \bigl\{\,
    \bigl( C_1, \dots, C_{i-1},\,\max\{C_i + b_j,\; A_i + a_j + b_j\},\,C_{i+1}, \dots, C_K,\; \\
    A_1, \dots, A_{i-1},\,A_i + a_j,\,A_{i+1}, \dots, A_K,\;\\
    B_1, \dots, B_{i-1},\,B_i + b_j,\,B_{i+1}, \dots, B_K \bigr)
\,\bigr\}
\end{align*}
Merge $i = 1,...,K$, to obtain $\mathscr{H}^{(j)}$. 
If we get the same tuples during the merge, store only one of them.

Once all jobs are assigned and each block $\mathcal{B}_i$ is summarized by its parameters $(A_i, B_i, C_i)$,
the overall makespan $C_{\max}$ is obtained by concatenating the blocks in order of increasing index.
The procedure is given below:

In the update of $T_B$,
the term $T_A - A_i$ is the start time of $\mathcal{B}_i$ on $M_1$, so $T_A - A_i + C_i$
is the completion time of $\mathcal{B}_i$ on $M_2$ when $M_2$ is free,
while $T_B + B_i$ accounts for the case where $M_2$ is the bottleneck.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Block parameters $(A_i,\, B_i\,)$ for $i = 1,\dots,K$.
    \Ensure Completion times $C_{\max}$.
    \State $T_A \gets 0$, \quad $T_B \gets 0$
    \For{$i = 1$ to $K$}
        \State $T_A^{new} \gets T_A^{old} + A_i$
        \State $T_B^{new} \gets \max\{\, T_A^{new} - A_i + C_i,\; T_B^{old} + B_i \,\}$
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $T_B$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{}
\label{alg:dp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Jobs $\mathcal{J}$ with $(a_j, b_j, w_j)$, numbered $1,\dots,n$ in Johnson order;
    \Ensure The optimal objective value $W C_{\max}^*$.
    \State $\mathscr{H}^{(0)} \gets \{ (0,\dots,0) \}$
    \For{$j = 1$ to $n$}
        \State $Y_i \gets \emptyset$ for $i = 1,\dots,K$
        \For{each $(C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}$}
            \For{$i = 1$ to $K$}
                \If{$w_j \le L_i$}
                   \State $C_i' \gets \max\{\, C_i + b_j,\; A_i + a_j + b_j \,\}$
                    \State $A_i' \gets A_i + a_j$
                    \State $B_i' \gets B_i + b_j$
                    \State Add $(\dots,C_i',\dots; \; \dots,A_i',\dots; \; \dots,B_i',\dots; \; )$ to $Y_i$
                \EndIf
               \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \State $\mathscr{H}^{(j)} \gets \textsc{Merge}(Y_1,\dots,Y_K)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:block-johnson}
    \EndFor
    \State $C_{\max} \gets +\infty$
    \For{each state $S \in \mathscr{H}$}
        \State $T_B \gets \textbf{Algorithm 1}\bigl( (A_1,\, B_1,\, C_1),\;\dots,\;(A_K,\, B_K,\, C_K) \bigr)$
        \State $C_{\max} \gets \min\{\, C_{\max},\; T_B \,\}$
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $C_{\max}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\vskip 4pt

The algorithm above computes the optimal makespan $C_{\max}$.
To obtain the weighted objective $W C_{\max}$ from a state $S \in \mathscr{H}$,
let $T_A^{(i)}$ and $T_B^{(i)}$ be the completion times on $M_1$ and $M_2$ after concatenating blocks
$\mathcal{B}_1$ through $\mathcal{B}_i$ via the Algorithmic~1.
Since blocks are concatenated in non-increasing weight order, $T_B^{(i)}$ is precisely the completion time
of the last job in $\mathcal{B}_i$.
The weighted completion time of the schedule corresponding to $S$ is therefore
\[
W C_{\max}(S) = \max_{i=1,\dots,K}\; L_i \cdot T_B^{(i)},
\]
where $L_i$ is the weight recorded for $\mathcal{B}_i$ (the weight of its last job).
Taking the minimum over all states in $\mathscr{H}$ yields the optimal objective value:
\[
W C_{\max}^* = \min_{S \in \mathscr{H}}\; \max_{i=1,\dots,K}\; L_i \cdot T_B^{(i)}.
\]


\begin{theorem}\label{thm:complexity}
Algorithm~2 solves $F2 \parallel W C_{\max}$ optimally in
$O\bigl(n \cdot K \cdot |\mathscr{H}|_{\max}\bigr)$ time, where
$|\mathscr{H}|_{\max} \le K^K \cdot V^{\,3K-2}$,
$V = \max\bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^n a_j,\;\sum_{j=1}^n b_j\bigr\}$.
For constant $K$, this is $O(n \cdot V^{\,3K-2})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each state in $\mathscr{H}^{(j)}$ is a $4K$-tuple
$(C_1,\dots,C_K;\, A_1,\dots,A_K;\, B_1,\dots,B_K;\, L_1,\dots,L_K)$.
We bound the number of distinct values each component can attain.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $A_i$ is the total $M_1$ processing time of jobs assigned to block $\mathcal{B}_i$,
        so $A_i \in [0,\,V]$. Since the job sequence is fixed and the $j$ jobs under
        consideration form a prefix, $\sum_{i=1}^K A_i$ equals the sum of $a_j$ over
        that prefix; consequently at most $K-1$ of the $A_i$ are independent,
        contributing $\le V^{\,K-1}$ distinct $A$-vectors.
  \item Symmetrically, $\sum_{i=1}^K B_i$ is the prefix sum of the $b_j$'s,
        so the $B$-vectors contribute $\le V^{\,K-1}$ distinct combinations.
  \item $C_i$ is the makespan of $\mathcal{B}_i$ scheduled in isolation
        according to Johnson's rule.  Since $C_i \le A_i + B_i \le 2V$,
        the $C$-vector contributes $\le (2V)^K$ distinct combinations.
  \item $L_i$ is the weight of the last job in $\mathcal{B}_i$ and takes values
        from $\{w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(K)}\}$, contributing $\le K^K$ distinct
        $L$-vectors.
\end{enumerate}
Multiplying these bounds yields
\[
|\mathscr{H}^{(j)}| \le K^K \cdot (2V)^K \cdot V^{\,2K-2}
                      = O\bigl(K^K \cdot 2^K \cdot V^{\,3K-2}\bigr).
\]

In iteration $j$, the algorithm takes each state in $\mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}$ and each
block $i \in \{1,\dots,K\}$, computes the updated tuple in $O(1)$ time,
and inserts it into $Y_i$.  The merge step collects the $K$ sets $Y_1,\dots,Y_K$
and removes duplicates, which can be done in time proportional to the total number
of generated tuples.  Hence iteration $j$ costs $O(K \cdot |\mathscr{H}^{(j-1)}|)$,
and the $n$ iterations together cost $O(n \cdot K \cdot |\mathscr{H}|_{\max})$.

The final concatenation (Algorithm~1) evaluates each state in $\mathscr{H}^{(n)}$
in $O(K)$ time, which is dominated by the DP phase.
When $K$ is fixed, $K^K \cdot 2^K$ is constant, giving $O(n \cdot V^{\,3K-2})$.
\end{proof}
