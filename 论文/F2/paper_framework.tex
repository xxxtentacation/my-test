\begin{abstract}
    Minimizing $w C_{\max}$ on a two-machine flow shop,
    denoted $F2 \parallel w C_{\max}$, is a classical NP-hard scheduling problem.
    Existing exact methods rely on enumerative techniques that scale poorly with instance size,
    while approximation algorithms often impose restrictive assumptions on processing times.
    We first sequence all jobs globally by Johnson's rule, obtaining an initial schedule $\pi$.
    Scanning $\pi$ from left to right, we partition it into at most $K$ blocks: whenever a weight not seen before appears, 
    it serves as the final job of the current block, and the next job starts a new block.
    We establish structural properties of optimal schedules: the permutation property, the positional constraint that block $\mathcal{G}_k$ jobs cannot appear beyond the first $k$ blocks,
    and the block-end condition requiring the final job of each block to carry that block's weight (automatically satisfied by construction).
    The intra-block Johnson ordering inherited from $\pi$ is further formalized within the DP framework.
    Building on these properties and the interval-based dynamic programming framework (\citealp{hall1998}) developed for $F2|r_j|C_{\max}$,
    we design a polynomial-time dynamic programming algorithm with $O(|Y|_{\max}\cdot n)$ complexity that appends jobs block by block.
    We further show that geometric rounding of weights and processing times converts this algorithm into a polynomial-time approximation scheme.
\end{abstract}

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} Flow shop scheduling; $w C_{\max}$; Dynamic programming; Structural properties

\section{NP-hard}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nphard}
    $F2 \parallel w C_{\max}$ is NP-hard.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We reduce from the Partition problem: given $n$ positive integers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_r$ with $\sum_{i=1}^{r} x_i = 2T$, 
does there exist subset $Z_1$ and $Z_2 \subseteq \{1,\dots,r\}$ such that:
 $\sum_{i \in Z_1} x_i$ = $\sum_{i \in Z_2} x_i$ = $T$?

Construct an instance of $F2 \parallel w C_{\max}$,  Let the number of jobs $n = r + 1$.
Let $a_j = 1$, $b_j = rx_j$, $w_j = T+1$ for jobs $j = 1,\dots,n-1$.
Put $a_n = rT$, $b_n = 1$, $w_n = 2T+1$ for job $J_n$.
Does there exist a threshold Y such that:
\[
w C_{\max} \le Y = r(T+1)(2T+1)
\]
holds?

We partition jobs $j_1$ to $j_{n-1}$ into two subsets via $j_n$, 
the distribution of jobs on the machine is($\mathcal{J}_{j\epsilon A_1}, J_n, \mathcal{J}_{j\epsilon A_2}$). 
Since the processing times of $A_1$ and $A_2$ are unknown, while their total processing time is 2T. 
We may assume that the processing time of $A_1$ on $M_2$ is $r(T+\xi)$, $A_2$ is $r(T-\xi)$, where $-T\le\xi\le T$.
Therefore, the completion time of $J_n$ is determined by the following formula:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completetime1}
    \qquad
    C_n^1 = |A_1| + rT + 1
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completetime2}
    \qquad
    C_n^2 = 1 + r ( T + \xi ) + 1
\end{equation}
and $C_n = \max \{C_n^1, C_n^2\}$

If $\xi = 0$, then $\sum_{j\epsilon A_1}b_j =\sum_{j\epsilon A_2}b_j $, which means the partition problem has a feasible solution. 
In this case, the completion time of job \(J_n\) is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime1}, we have:
\begin{align*}
w_nC_n &= (2T+1)(|A_1| + rT + 1) \\
       &\leqslant (2T+1)(rT + r) \\
       &= r(T+1)(2T+1) \\
       &= Y\\
wC_{\max} &= r(T+1)(C_n + rT) = Y
\end{align*}
so $w_jC_j \leqslant wC_{\max} = Y$

If the scheduling problem exists a threshold y such that \(wC_{\text{max}} \le y\).
When $ \xi > 0 $, $C_n$is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime2}, we have:
\begin{align*}
w_nC_n &= (1 + r ( T + \xi ) + 1)(2T + 1) \\
        &\geqslant (1 + r ( T + 1 ) + 1)(2T + 1) \\
        &> Y
\end{align*}
When $ \xi < 0 $, $C_n$is determined by \eqref{eq:completetime1}, we have:
\begin{align*}
wC_{\max} &= (C_n +  \sum_{j\epsilon A_2}b_j)(T+1)  \\
         &= (1 + rT + |A_1|  + r ( T - \xi ))(T+1) \\
         &\geqslant (rT + r + rT)(T+1)  \\
         &= r(T+1)(2T+1) = Y
\end{align*}
so $wC_{\max} > Y$, there exists a contradiction.


Therefore, the Partition instance admits a solution if and only if 
the constructed $F2 \parallel w C_{\max}$ instance admits a schedule with $w C_{\max} \le Y$.
    
\end{proof}



\section{\texorpdfstring{$F2 \parallel w C_{\max}$}{F2 || w C\_max} for the number of weights is constant} 
\label{sec:dp_algorithm}

When the number of distinct weights $K$ is bounded by a constant, 
the structural properties established in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} enable a polynomial-time dynamic programming algorithm.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:johnson}, the initial job sequence follows Johnson's rule; 
the block partition and all subsequent steps are defined with respect to this Johnson order.
We first describe the block partition and group logic underlying our approach, 
then present the DP formulation, and finally give the complete algorithm and its complexity analysis.

\subsection{Algorithm Properties and Dynamic Programming}

The jobs are partitioned into blocks by a single left-to-right scan. 
Starting from the first job, a new block is created whenever a weight value not encountered before appears; 
that job becomes the final job of the current block, and the next job starts a new block. 
Since each block introduces at least one previously unseen weight, the procedure yields at most $K$ blocks, 
denoted $\mathcal{B}_1, \mathcal{B}_2, \dots, \mathcal{B}_K$. By construction, 
the final job of each block carries the weight that triggered the cut.
By construction, block weights are non-increasing, 
i.e., $W(\mathcal{B}_1) \ge W(\mathcal{B}_2) \ge \cdots \ge W(\mathcal{B}_K)$. 

Jobs of the same weight form a natural group, denoted $\mathcal{G}_1, \mathcal{G}_2, \dots, \mathcal{G}_K$. 
Scheduling heavier groups before lighter ones automatically enforces the non-increasing block-weight condition, 
and scanning groups in descending weight order directly yields the block partition.

\begin{exam}\label{ex:blocking}
Consider a 6-job instance with Johnson sequence $\pi = (J_1, \dots, J_6)$ and weights
$(w_1,\dots,w_6) = (2,\,2,\,5,\,2,\,4,\,2)$.
Scanning $\pi$ from left to right and cutting whenever a weight not seen before appears yields the following partition:
\[
\underbrace{J_1,\; J_2\; J_3}_{\mathcal{B}_1}\;
\underbrace{J_4,\; J_5}_{\mathcal{B}_2}\;
\underbrace{J_6}_{\mathcal{B}_3}
\qquad
\]
The resulting blocks are $\mathcal{B}_1=\{J_1,J_2,J_3\}$, $\mathcal{B}_2=\{J_4,J_5\}$, $\mathcal{B}_3=\{J_6\}$. 
And $\mathcal{G}_1=\{J_1,J_2,J_4,J_6\}$, $\mathcal{G}_2=\{J_5\}$, $\mathcal{G}_3=\{J_3\}$.
\end{exam}

\label{sec:dp_formulation}

We now develop a dynamic programming algorithm that processes jobs sequentially in Johnson order
and assigns each job to one of the $K$ blocks $\mathcal{B}_1,\dots,\mathcal{B}_K$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:block-johnson} provides the structural basis:
it guarantees that, given any assignment of jobs to the $K$ blocks,
sequencing the jobs within each block $\mathcal{B}_i$ according to Johnson's rule
and concatenating the blocks in non-increasing weight order yields a schedule
whose makespan is no larger than that of any other schedule respecting the same assignment.
We denote by $\mathscr{H}$ the set of states maintained during the computation;
each state summarizes the aggregate parameters of all $K$ blocks after a prefix of jobs has been processed.

\paragraph{State Definition.}

A state is a $4K$-tuple
\[
    (C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}
\]
where for each block $\mathcal{B}_i$, $i = 1,\dots,K$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $A_i$ --- total processing time on machine $M_1$ of jobs assigned to $\mathcal{B}_i$;
    \item $B_i$ --- total processing time on machine $M_2$ of jobs assigned to $\mathcal{B}_i$;
    \item $C_i$ --- makespan of $\mathcal{B}_i$ when the jobs assigned to it are scheduled in isolation;
    \item $L_i$ --- weight of the last job in $\mathcal{B}_i$ (or $0$ if $\mathcal{B}_i$ is empty).
\end{itemize}

The initial state set contains a single state with all components set to zero:
$\mathscr{H}^{(0)} = \{ (0,\dots,0) \}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dominance}
Let $S$ and $S'$ be two states in $\mathscr{H}$ after processing the same prefix of jobs.
If $A_i \le A_i'$, $B_i \le B_i'$, $C_i \le C_i'$, and $L_i \le L_i'$ for all $i = 1,\dots,K$, 
then $S'$ is dominated by $S$ and can be removed from further consideration.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let the remaining jobs be processed identically from both states.
By the monotonicity of the recurrence in~\eqref{eq:trans_C} and the concatenation formula,
smaller $A_i$, $B_i$, $C_i$ values lead to no larger final makespan.
Smaller $L_i$ ensures the weighted completion time objective is not increased.
Hence $S$ yields a schedule no worse than $S'$ under $w C_{\max}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{State Transition.}

For $j = 1,\dots,n$, we generate $\mathscr{H}^{(k)}$ from $\mathscr{H}^{(k-1)}$ as follows.
Set $\mathcal{B}_i = \emptyset$ for $i = 1,\dots,K$.
For each state $(C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}^{(k-1)}$
and each block index $i = 1,\dots,K$.
Job $J_j$ may be assigned to the first i blocks whose index does not exceed the rank of its weight,
then add a new state to $\mathcal{B}_i$ with the $i$-th block updated as follows:
\begin{align}
    A_i' &= A_i + a_j, \label{eq:trans_A}\\
    B_i' &= B_i + b_j, \label{eq:trans_B}\\
    C_i' &= \max\{\, C_i + b_j,\; A_i + a_j + b_j \,\}, \label{eq:trans_C}\\
    L_i' &= w_j. \label{eq:trans_L}
\end{align}
All other components remain unchanged.
Merge $\mathcal{B}_1, \dots, \mathcal{B}_K$ and apply the dominance rule
(Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance}) to obtain $\mathscr{H}^{(k)}$.



Once all jobs are assigned and each block $\mathcal{B}_i$ is summarized by its parameters $(A_i, B_i, C_i)$,
the overall makespan $C_{\max}$ is obtained by concatenating the blocks in order of increasing index.
The procedure is given below:

In the update of $T_B$,
the term $T_A - A_i$ is the start time of $\mathcal{B}_i$ on $M_1$, so $T_A - A_i + C_i$
is the completion time of $\mathcal{B}_i$ on $M_2$ when $M_2$ is free,
while $T_B + B_i$ accounts for the case where $M_2$ is the bottleneck.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Block parameters $(A_i,\, B_i,\, C_i)$ for $i = 1,\dots,K$.
    \Ensure Completion times $(T_A,\, T_B)$ after concatenating all $K$ blocks, where $T_B = C_{\max}$.
    \State $T_A \gets 0$, \quad $T_B \gets 0$
    \For{$i = 1$ to $K$}
        \State $T_A \gets T_A + A_i$
        \State $T_B \gets \max\{\, T_A - A_i + C_i,\; T_B + B_i \,\}$
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $(T_A,\; T_B)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{}
\label{alg:dp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Jobs $\mathcal{J}$ with $(a_j, b_j, w_j)$, numbered $1,\dots,n$ in Johnson order.
    \Ensure The optimal makespan $C_{\max}$.
    \State $\mathscr{H} \gets \{ (0,\dots,0) \}$
    \For{$j = 1$ to $n$}
        \State $\mathscr{Y}_i \gets \emptyset$ for $i = 1,\dots,K$
        \For{each $(C_1,\dots,C_K; \; A_1,\dots,A_K; \; B_1,\dots,B_K; \; L_1,\dots,L_K) \in \mathscr{H}$}
            \For{$i = 1$ to $\ell(j)$}
                \State $A_i' \gets A_i + a_j$
                \State $B_i' \gets B_i + b_j$
                \State $C_i' \gets \max\{\, C_i + b_j,\; A_i + a_j + b_j \,\}$
                \State $L_i' \gets w_j$
                \State Add $(\dots,C_i',\dots; \; \dots,A_i',\dots; \; \dots,B_i',\dots; \; \dots,L_i',\dots)$ to $\mathscr{Y}_i$
            \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \State $\mathscr{H} \gets \textsc{Merge}(\mathscr{Y}_1,\dots,\mathscr{Y}_K)$
    \EndFor
    \State $C_{\max} \gets +\infty$
    \For{each state $S \in \mathscr{H}$}
        \State $(T_A,\, T_B) \gets \textbf{Algorithm 1}\bigl( (A_1,\, B_1,\, C_1),\;\dots,\;(A_K,\, B_K,\, C_K) \bigr)$
        \State $C_{\max} \gets \min\{\, C_{\max},\; T_B \,\}$
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $C_{\max}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\vskip 4pt

The algorithm above computes the optimal makespan $C_{\max}$.
To obtain the weighted objective $wC_{\max}$ from a state $S \in \mathscr{H}$,
let $T_A^{(i)}$ and $T_B^{(i)}$ be the completion times on $M_1$ and $M_2$ after concatenating blocks
$\mathcal{B}_1$ through $\mathcal{B}_i$ via the Algorithmic~1.
Since blocks are concatenated in non-increasing weight order, $T_B^{(i)}$ is precisely the completion time
of the last job in $\mathcal{B}_i$.
The weighted completion time of the schedule corresponding to $S$ is therefore
\[
wC_{\max}(S) = \max_{i=1,\dots,K}\; L_i \cdot T_B^{(i)},
\]
where $L_i$ is the weight recorded for $\mathcal{B}_i$ (the weight of its last job).
Taking the minimum over all states in $\mathscr{H}$ yields the optimal objective value:
\[
wC_{\max}^* = \min_{S \in \mathscr{H}}\; \max_{i=1,\dots,K}\; L_i \cdot T_B^{(i)}.
\]
Equivalently, this computation can be folded into Step~3 of Algorithm~$\mathscr{H}$
by updating $\text{obj} \gets \max\{\,\text{obj},\; L_i \cdot T_B\,\}$ after each Algorithmic~1 iteration
and returning $\min_{S} \text{obj}$ instead of $C_{\max}$.
