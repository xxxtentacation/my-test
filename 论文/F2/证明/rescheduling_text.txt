Abstract
This paper addresses a rescheduling problem where a set of original jobs has already
been scheduled to optimize a speciﬁc objective on a single machine. However, before
processing begins, a set of new jobs arrives and should be inserted into the original
jobs with minimal disruption to the planned schedule, which creates a necessity for
rescheduling. The disruption is quantiﬁed by the maximum deviation in completion
time for any original job between the planned and the rescheduled sequences. To
ensure an acceptable service level for the original jobs, the rescheduling process allows
for the rejection of some new jobs, with each rejected job paying a corresponding
rejection cost. We explore three general models in which the maximum completion
time disruption is considered both as a constraint and as a component of the cost
objective. The primary scheduling objective is to minimize the maximum delivery
completion time. The overall cost objective is to minimize the sum of the scheduling
objective for the original and newly accepted jobs, the maximum disruption penalty,
and the total rejection cost. We present a corresponding dynamic programming exact
algorithm that runs in pseudo-polynomial time. Additionally, by employing a trimming
technique, we propose a fully polynomial-time approximation scheme.
Keywords Rescheduling · Single machine · Job rejection · Dynamic programming ·
Approximation scheme · Delivery time

1 Introduction
Rescheduling, which involves adjusting a planned schedule to accommodate
unforeseen disruptive events, has been extensively studied in industries such as man-
ufacturing and healthcare [1–3, 19, 20]. Common disruptive events include the arrival
of new orders [ 8], delays in some jobs [ 7], and periods of machine unavailability due
to breakdowns [ 10]. In this work, we focus on rescheduling when unexpected jobs,
referred to as new jobs, must be inserted into an already planned schedule consisting
of known jobs, referred to as original jobs. To limit disruption to the planned schedule
and maintain an acceptable service level, the decision maker may reject some of the
new jobs in order to optimize a speciﬁed objective. Our problem falls within the scope
of rescheduling for new jobs with job rejection.
V arious rescheduling problems involving new jobs have been widely investigated.
Hall and Potts [8] addressed several single-machine rescheduling problems involving
new jobs, focusing on minimizing total completion time or maximum lateness, subject
to disruption constraints or costs. They derived polynomial-time algorithms or proved
NP-hardness for the problems considered. Teghem and Tuyttens [14] further examined
these problems by exploring the enumeration of Pareto-optimal solutions. Zhao et al.
[21] introduced a more generalized disruption criterion by imposing a maximum allow-
able disruption for each original job. They proved strong NP-hardness for minimizing
the maximum lateness and presented a simple 2-approximation polynomial-time algo-
rithm. For minimizing total completion time, they also provided a strong NP-hardness
proof and developed a heuristic algorithm. Additionally, by relaxing the maximum
allowable disruptions, they proposed a super-optimal dual-approximation polynomial-
time algorithm. Hall et al. [ 9] considered rescheduling with multiple sets of new jobs
arriving at different times. For minimizing maximum lateness subject to a customer
service requirement, modeled as a limit on the maximum change in completion time for
the original jobs, they developed approximation algorithms and analyzed their worst-
case performances. They also proposed a branch-and-bound algorithm and conducted
numerical experiments on instances with up to 1 000 jobs. For the related surveys up
to 2009, we refer readers to Vieira et al. [ 17] and Ouelhadj and Petrovic [ 13]. After
that, Guo et al. [5] examined a single-machine rescheduling problem for newly arrived
rework jobs, aiming to minimize total waiting time while achieving energy savings.
They formulated the problem as a mixed-integer linear program and proved its NP-
hardness. They also developed a pseudo-polynomial optimal algorithm for a special
case, alongside heuristic and genetic algorithms with adaptive local search for the
general case, demonstrating their effectiveness through computational experiments.
Zhang [22] focused on rescheduling problems involving the arrival of unexpected new
jobs, aiming to minimize maximum weighted tardiness costs while limiting disruptions
to the original schedule. They proved the problem’s strong NP-hardness and presented
a 2-approximation polynomial-time algorithm, as well as three local search strategies
to improve the approximate solution, followed by numerical experiments to evaluate
their performance. Rener et al. [ 15] studied a rescheduling problem for minimizing

maximum lateness, with a constraint on total time disruption, and presented the ﬁrst
exact algorithm to solve the problem with an additional no-idle constraint. Wang et al.
[18] were among the ﬁrst to integrate rescheduling for new jobs with job rejection into
a uniﬁed model. They described a multi-objective dynamic rescheduling problem in
response to continuous job arrivals, under the assumption that jobs could be rejected

integrated cost function considered total completion time, total rejection cost, and
resource consumption cost, with disruption penalties measured by total virtual tardi-

disruption costs. Fang et al. [ 12] studied rescheduling with job rejection to minimize
a linear combination of total weighted completion time, maximum disruption, and
rejection cost. They proved NP-hardness for the unit weight case and developed an
exact dynamic programming algorithm, as well as a fully polynomial-time approxi-
mation scheme (FPTAS). Their proposed algorithms were demonstrated to be efﬁcient
through computational experiments. Lendl et al. [ 11] studied rescheduling problems
due to the arrival of new orders in which these new jobs should be integrated in a
proper way in the existing schedule of the so-called old jobs, with the aim of min-
imizing an objective function for the joint set of jobs under the constraint that each
old job is not allowed to deviate from its original completion time by more than a
certain threshold. For the objective of minimizing the total weighted completion time,
they showed that the resulting rescheduling problem is weakly NP-hard and derived an
exact dynamic programming algorithm with pseudo-polynomial running time. A fully
polynomial-time approximation scheme was obtained from the dynamic program by
three different scaling and reduction steps. For the objective of minimizing the number
of late jobs, a strong NP-hardness proof was given. Rener et al. [16] presented a whole
classiﬁcation of rescheduling problems, with several objective functions and two time
disruption constraints, based on the presence of idle times in optimal solutions. The
need to insert idle times was shown to be caused by a disruption of the original order of
old jobs, which generally makes these problems more difﬁcult to solve than problems
where the original order of old jobs is preserved. Several examples and properties
related to the insertion of idle times were provided, along with complexity results for
ﬁve problem variants.
In this paper, we study a novel rescheduling problem where a set of original jobs
has already been scheduled on a single machine to minimize the maximum delivery
completion time. However, before formal processing begins, a set of new jobs arrives
and should be inserted into the original schedule without excessively disrupting the
planned schedule, with disruption measured as the maximum deviation in completion
time for any given original job between the planned and adjusted schedules. To main-
tain an acceptable service level for the original jobs, the decision maker may reject
some new jobs, incurring rejection costs. We examine three general models where
the disruption is treated both as a constraint and as part of the cost objective, with
the primary scheduling objective being the minimization of the maximum delivery
completion time. The overall cost objective is to minimize the sum of the scheduling

In this section, we formally deﬁne the studied rescheduling problem as follows.
Let JO ={ 1, 2,··· , nO} represent a set of original non-preemptive jobs to be
processed on a single machine. Before processing begins, these original jobs have been
arranged in an optimal planned schedule to minimize a given objective function, with
no-idle time between the jobs. A set of new non-preemptive jobs,J

the total operational cost, which comprises the scheduling objective, the disruption
penalty, and the rejection cost.

6 Concluding Remarks
In this paper, we address the rescheduling problem on a single machine, focusing
on the impact of newly arriving jobs with the option for job rejection. The primary
scheduling objective is to minimize the maximum delivery completion time. We pro-
pose a novel general rescheduling model where the total cost includes the scheduling
objective for the original jobs and the newly accepted jobs, a disruption penalty, and
the total rejection cost, subject to a maximum allowable disruption threshold. To
solve this problem, we derive a pseudo-polynomial-time dynamic programming exact
algorithm. Additionally, we present a fully polynomial-time approximation scheme
(FPTAS). Future research could explore extensions of this model to different machine
environments, such as ﬂow shops or parallel machines, or consider other types of
disruptions that may arise in more complex scheduling scenarios.
