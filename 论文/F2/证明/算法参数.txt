
The genetic algorithm was calibrated before the study, and each of its parameters is a function
of the instance size, so that one rule covers both scales: its crossover probability is
$p_c=\min(1,\,19/20+n/4000)$ and its mutation probability $p_m=\min(1,\,1/2+n/500)$, the
mutation doubling its share over the scale while the crossover starts near one.
Three settings the scales do not share. The number of generations is
$G=\max(2,\lfloor n/4\rfloor)$ on the small scale and $G=\max(16,\lfloor n^{2}/2000\rfloor)$ on
the large one, the number of offspring a generation produces is
$\lambda=\max(8,\lfloor n^{2}/12\rfloor)$ on the small scale and
$\lambda=\min(200,60+\lfloor n/2\rfloor)$ on the large one, and the local search of
\Cref{alg:ga} reinserts $k=\max(1,\lfloor n/8\rfloor)$ jobs on the small scale and
$k=\max(2,\lfloor 12n/100\rfloor-10)$ on the large one, so that its width grows faster than the
instance.
Since Johnson's rule carries no weight, the three settings above say how much of the search is
spent building the criterion into the schedule. The two scales are therefore read for different
things: the small one for the course of the search, whose settings are the narrow ones and whose
gap against the optimum falls from $17$ per cent at ten jobs to $4$ at sixty, closing on the
construction rule without passing it; the large one for what the search reaches when it is given
room, its settings growing faster than the instance and its gap closing as the instance grows.
